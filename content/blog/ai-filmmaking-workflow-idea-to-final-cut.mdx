The difference between an amateur experimenting with generative prompts and a professional filmmaker is having a repeatable, disciplined production workflow. Without a system, AI video creation devolves into an expensive game of slot-machine prompting where assets fail to connect and projects are abandoned.

This guide details a proven 5-phase workflow you can execute from idea to final cut.

## Phase 1: Pre-Production & Scripting

Everything in filmmaking begins with story architecture. Trying to prompt visual shots before locking your script leads to wasted generation credits.

1. **Premise & Logline:** Use Claude to brainstorm core story hooks, high-concept premises, and antagonist conflicts.
2. **Three-Act Beat Sheet:** Define clear turning points (Inciting Incident, Midpoint, Climax, Resolution).
3. **Screenplay Drafting:** Format each scene with precise sluglines (e.g., `EXT. CYBERPUNK ALLEYWAY - NIGHT`) and detailed sensory action descriptions.

Structuring your prompts like a professional camera operator guarantees far more intentional, usable generations than improvised freeform prompting.

---

## Phase 2: Asset Pre-Visualization & Visual Continuity

Consistency is the benchmark of professional production. Before generating a single second of video, you must establish locked visual references.

### Character Model Sheets
- Generate full-body turnaround plates of your key characters against neutral studio backdrops.
- Note specific prompt keywords that anchor their likeness (e.g., *sharp angular jawline, messy silver undercut, dark trench coat, emerald eyes*).
- Save these reference plates in high resolution to use across image-to-video pipelines.

### Location & Lighting Concept Plates
- Generate establishing shots of every key setting in your film.
- Keep color palettes consistent: establish whether a scene uses cold fluorescent lighting, warm amber tungsten, or moody natural sunlight. Re-using environment anchor plates ensures the viewer always recognizes the world.

---

## Phase 3: Generative Video Production

With your asset sheets locked, begin the video generation stage using models such as Runway Gen-3, Kling AI, or Seedance.

1. **Image-to-Video First:** Upload your pre-visualized character or location plate as the starting frame. This locks your aesthetic before motion begins.
2. **Direct the Camera, Not Just the Subject:** Prompt explicit camera movements:
   - *Static camera with slow atmospheric dust particles moving right*
   - *Slow tracking shot following character walking down hallway*
   - *Fast whip-pan left to reveal empty doorway*
3. **Motion Brush & Dynamic Sliders:** If available in your chosen tool, use motion brushes to paint motion only onto water, hair, or vehicles while keeping characters stable.
4. **Curate the Best Takes:** Expect to generate 3 to 5 iterations per shot to achieve natural, glitch-free motion. Save your best takes into organized scene folders. Test low-motion values first before dialing up dramatic movement.

## Phase 4: Audio Engineering & Voice Acting

Sound design gives synthetic imagery believable physical weight.

- **Character Voices:** Synthesize dialogue in ElevenLabs. Add subtle pauses, ellipses, and commas to pace character breath.
- **Environmental Ambience:** Never leave a scene silent. Build multi-track stems with room tone, outdoor wind, distant traffic, and floor creaks.
- **Original Scoring:** Compose thematic soundtracks using Suno. Match the musical tempo (BPM) and instrumentation to the dramatic tension of the scene.

---

## Phase 5: Editorial Assembly & Finishing

The final step takes place in an NLE like DaVinci Resolve or Adobe Premiere Pro.

1. **Rough Assembly:** Drop your dialogue and key shots into the timeline to verify pacing and scene length.
2. **Action Trimming:** Trim the initial 0.5s and final 0.5s of AI generation clips to eliminate morphing artifacts or static freeze frames.
3. **Sound Mixing:** Level dialogue clear above the music track, side-chaining the score during spoken lines.
4. **Unifying Color Grade:** Apply a subtle film grain overlay and unified 35mm LUT to blend disparate model outputs into a cohesive cinematic aesthetic. A unified color profile pulls disparate AI generations together like a real camera sensor.
