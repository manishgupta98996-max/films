## 2. Runway Gen-3 Motion Architecture: How the Engine Reads Prompts

Runway Gen-3 Alpha is trained on millions of hours of cinematic footage, documentary film archives, and physical camera metadata. 

However, the model processes prompt tokens hierarchically. If you bury your camera instructions at the end of a long, flowery paragraph, the model's text encoder deprioritizes the camera vector in favor of the visual subject descriptors.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  RUNWAY GEN-3 PROMPT TOKEN HIERARCHY                     |
|                                                                          |
|  [HIGH ATTENTION TOKENS: POSITIONS 1 - 20]                               |
|  ├── Shot Framing & Primary Lens (e.g., "Cinematic medium close-up, 50mm")|
|  └── Primary Camera Vector (e.g., "Slow camera dolly in along Z-axis")   |
|                                                                          |
|  [MEDIUM ATTENTION TOKENS: POSITIONS 21 - 50]                            |
|  ├── Subject & Motivated Action (e.g., "Astronaut inspects ancient relic")|
|  └── Secondary Physics (e.g., "Dust motes drifting through light beam")  |
|                                                                          |
|  [ENVIRONMENT & TEXTURE TOKENS: POSITIONS 51+]                           |
|  └── Optical Profile & Film Grain (e.g., "24fps, Kodak 5219 film stock") |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The Three Golden Rules of Runway Motion

1. **Front-Load the Camera Instruction:** Always place your camera type, lens focal length, and movement vector in the first ten words of your prompt.
2. **Use Mechanical Film Terminology:** Avoid vague emotional prose (*"dramatic epic view"*). Use precise mechanical cinema terms: *"dolly in"*, *"pedestal down"*, *"tracking follow"*, *"anamorphic 35mm"*, *"shutter angle 180 degrees"*.
3. **Always Use Image-to-Video:** When you generate from text alone, Runway must invent both the physical geometry and the motion simultaneously. When you upload a locked starting plate (Image-to-Video), the engine dedicates 100% of its compute power to animating camera physics and lighting, resulting in 4x cleaner motion.

### 1. The Slow Psychological Push-In (The Suspense Dolly)
Use this move when a character realizes a terrible truth, makes a pivotal decision, or when tension is escalating in a quiet room.

```text
[Prompt Block: Slow Psychological Dolly In]
Prompt: Cinematic medium close-up, slow steady camera dolly push-in along the Z-axis toward the subject's face. 50mm prime anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field, sharp focus on subject's intense eyes, blurred background. High-contrast low-key chiaroscuro lighting, subtle split rim light. Subject maintains still, focused facial expression with subtle breathing. Smooth mechanical dolly track movement, zero camera shake, 24fps motion blur, 35mm film grain.
Director Mode Settings: Zoom Z: +2.0 | Pan X: 0 | Tilt Y: 0 | Speed: 2.5
Negative Prompt: fast movement, shaky camera, zooming, digital distortion, cartoon, blurry eyes, morphing.
```

### 4. The Vertigo / Hitchcock Dolly Zoom (The Psychological Shock)

```text

Prompt: Cinematic medium shot executing a classic Hitchcock vertigo dolly zoom effect. The camera physically dollies backward while the lens focal length optically compresses, causing the background walls to dramatically warp and stretch inward. The central character remains perfectly locked in scale and position in the center of the frame. High-contrast split lighting, psychological dread, 50mm to 100mm optical compression transition, 24fps film cadence.
Director Mode Settings: Zoom Z: -3.5 | In-Frame Subject Lock: Enabled | Speed: 3.5
Negative Prompt: character shrinking, camera panning, shaky frame, morphing facial features, distorted eyes.
```

## 4. Mastering Motion Brush 2.0: Isolating Movement Without Morphing

One of the most revolutionary tools inside Runway Gen-3 is **Motion Brush 2.0**. 

In older models, if you prompted for *"rain falling and wind blowing"*, the model would often blow the actor's face across the screen like melted wax. Motion Brush allows you to isolate and paint up to **five independent motion layers** across your starting frame.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  MOTION BRUSH 2.0: MULTI-LAYER STACK                     |
|                                                                          |
|  LAYER 1: BACKGROUND ATMOSPHERE (Wind / Fog / Rain)                      |
|  • Paint brush over sky and mist. Set X: +2.0 (Gentle lateral drift).    |
|                                                                          |
|  LAYER 2: SECONDARY SUBJECT PHYSICS (Cloth / Hair / Fire)                |
|  • Paint brush over cloak and campfire. Set Y: +1.5 (Vertical flicker).  |
|                                                                          |
|  LAYER 3: STATIC ANCHOR (Character Face & Eyes)                          |
|  • DO NOT PAINT. Leaving regions unbrushed acts as a mathematical        |
|    anchor, forcing the facial bone structure to remain 100% frozen.      |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The "Unbrushed Anchor" Technique
The most important rule of Motion Brush is what you **do not paint**:
- When directing an actor in a dialogue scene, **never paint the Motion Brush over their eyes, nose, or mouth**.
- Paint the motion brush over their jacket (to simulate breathing), over their hair (to simulate wind), and over the background trees.
- By leaving the face unbrushed, you instruct Runway's spatial diffusion engine to preserve the exact facial geometry of your starting frame, completely eliminating facial melting!

## 6. Negative Prompts & Artifact Traps: What to Block

Negative prompts in Runway act as mathematical guardrails, steering the diffusion denoising process away from common failure modes.

Always paste this standardized **Cinematic Negative Prompt Stack** into your Runway settings:

```text
[Universal Runway Negative Prompt Stack]
morphing, facial distortion, melting skin, extra eyes, duplicate limbs, rubbery legs, erratic camera spin, tilted horizon, jerky frame skips, digital sharpening artifacts, videogame CGI, cartoon, anime, oversaturated colors, floating objects, disconnected hands.
```

### Three Common Artifact Traps and How to Avoid Them
1. **The Rubber Leg Trap:** If an actor walks toward the camera too quickly, their knees will bend in unnatural directions. **Fix:** Lower the camera speed slider in Director Mode to below `3.5` and use a wider 24mm lens.
2. **The Horizontal Line Warp:** Rapid panning shots across slatted window blinds or tiled brick walls can cause visual "jello" warping. **Fix:** Lower horizontal pan velocity and set your shutter angle prompt to `180 degrees`.
3. **The Disappearing Prop Trap:** If a character holds a small prop (like a keycard or scanner), it will often dissolve between frames. **Fix:** Use Motion Brush to anchor the hand and prop with a static velocity setting.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Runway Camera Prompts

### How do I make the camera move smoothly in Runway Gen-3?
Specify precise mechanical camera vectors in your prompt (e.g., 'smooth slow camera dolly in along Z-axis at steady walking pace, 50mm prime lens, 24fps motion blur') and avoid vague words like 'cinematic' or 'epic'.

### How do I execute a Hitchcock vertigo dolly zoom in Runway Gen-3?
Prompt a simultaneous contra-move: 'Dolly zoom effect, camera dollies backward while background perspective expands, lens focal length compresses, dramatic psychological vertigo distortion, foreground character remains stationary'.

### Why does my character's body warp when the camera moves fast?
High camera speed combined with complex human action overwhelms the diffusion attention layers. Keep camera motion values moderate (speed 2 to 4 in Director Mode) and isolate head and body movement using Motion Brush 2.0.

### Should I use text-to-video or image-to-video for camera movement in Runway?
Always use Image-to-Video. Feeding a clean, locked starting frame allows Runway to dedicate 100% of its generative compute to simulating camera physics and motion cadence, eliminating facial drift.
